## Where candidates come from

Any public repository. The sandbox is behind you; the field is now
all of GitHub, and your fit profile below is what keeps that
searchable. Start from repos in languages and tools you named there,
and let the profile steer where you search, not just how results are
ranked.

**Claims count again.** The Path Review house rule does not travel.
Out here a claim comment or an assignee is a stranger who got there
first, and you have no channel to coordinate with them. Read claim
signals exactly as the rubric's checks state them; do not carry the
classroom habit of claiming over someone.

**Claim upstream the moment you commit.** The rule cuts both ways:
the instant you decide to take an issue, say so on the issue itself
(a short comment: what you plan to do and roughly when). You are now
the claim signal someone else's skill will read. Deciding privately
and claiming later is how two strangers do the same work twice.

I have experience with Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, and Java/C#, and I'm comfortable across the full stack — frontend, backend, and the glue in between, including both SQL (Postgres/MySQL/SQL Server) and NoSQL (MongoDB/DynamoDB) databases, AWS cloud infrastructure, and CI/CD pipelines. For this course I want to get better at the open-source contribution workflow itself (reading unfamiliar codebases, working through issue-to-PR the right way) and at using AI-assisted engineering effectively on real work. I'd like to avoid issues that require a language I don't already know, that need deep spelunking through a large, legacy codebase to even understand the fix, or that live in repos that look inactive or unresponsive. I do not want documentation-only issues: I want a change to code. Any kind of code change is fine (bug fix, tests for real logic, small feature, performance or security fix) in any kind of project (web, CLI, data or cloud, AI tooling). I can give about a weekend (8 to 10 hours) to the fix. The quality of the issue matters more than the size of the repo, but the repo must be active. I expect a good issue to state the expected behavior and to name the file or error to change, or to give steps to reproduce, so I can start without guessing, and the work should take about 8 to 10 hours.
